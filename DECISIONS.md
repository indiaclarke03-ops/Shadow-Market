# Build decisions (for India's review)

Judgement calls made while building from `specification.md`. Spec §12 decisions are unchanged. Each line below is open to reversal.

## 2026-11-15
1. **Layer 3 is pending, not estimated.** Sayari was not connected in the build session, so every Layer 3 component (readout, flow volumes, exposure curves, exporter table) renders an explicit "awaiting Sayari snapshot" state. No illustrative numbers are shown as data.
2. **Simulation runs on labelled placeholders.** The flagged share at each threshold, and most input ranges, are structural placeholders with a visible "placeholder" badge and a "scenario, not forecast" banner. The release rate is calibrated from CBP once Layer 2 is captured.
3. **Simulation threshold ladder.** The dial space is collapsed into six cumulative steps for the threshold curve: T0 listed → T1 direct (≈ current) → T2 tiers 2–3 → T3 tiers 2–4 product → T4 + one-hop ownership → T5 + research-named. This order is my choice; the spec doesn't fix one.
4. **Hidden exposure scales with depth.** Untraced exposure is surfaced in proportion to rule depth (0, .25, .5, .75, .9, 1 for T0…T5). This is a structural assumption, shown in the Model box.
5. **Shortage duration model.** shortage = max(0, ramp + gap/capacity − buffer×100/gap). It's simple and stated on the page.
6. **The copula couples only hidden exposure and rerouting** (ρ = 0.5, stated assumption), as in the spec's correlation example.
7. **Layer 2 solar proxy.** HTS 8541 is used if CBP figures at that level are found (the 2026 dashboard has HTS-4 filters). Otherwise Electronics. Both are labelled as upper bounds; HTS 8541 also covers LEDs, diodes and other semiconductors.
8. **R1 research-named flags take their tier from the factor name** (e.g. `forced_labor_aspi_origin_subtier` counts at E2 under the research scope), so the tier-depth and trigger-scope dials compose.
9. **Corroboration moves confidence by one named level** (e.g. medium → high), per spec §3.
10. **Flow diagram.** The last column is "left on the US market if flagged supply is removed", drawn at absolute scale so the drop is visible. The lavender band is supply flagged only at deeper settings: the shadow market itself.
11. **Design.** Taken from the reference screenshots: indigo/periwinkle striped hero, ice-blue panels, Inter Tight display (tight tracking) with Source Sans 3 body. No mono or condensed fonts. Exposure colours were checked with the dataviz palette validator (A1/R1 pass with labels as secondary encoding).
12. **Separate git repo.** `git init` was run inside the project folder because the home directory is itself a repo (remote EvacSimulator). Remote: github.com/indiaclarke03-ops/Shadow-Market.

## 2026-11-16
13. **Release rate by value, not count.** The Oct 2026 CBP dashboard's all-industry line counts (15,547 released) are lower than the Nov 2025 guide's (39,829). Counts cannot fall, so the definition changed. Values agree across both cuts, so `build.py` computes released ÷ (released + denied) by value (HTS 8541: 79.5%) and shows the count rate (65.4%) as secondary. See `pipeline/notes/layer2_research.md`.
14. **C-018 resolved.** Xinte Energy is not named in the Aug 2026 Entity List notice (L2-032) or on the list page (L2-022). The claim now says so, with those sources added; confidence stays medium.
15. **Layer 3 is measured in shipments, not dollars.** Sayari's US import records are bills of lading with weight but no declared value (raw: `search_shipments_first-solar_…`). Market shares, curves and cliffs use counts of US-bound shipment records for HS 8541.42/.43 over the 730-day window. Tolerated exposure (Layer 3 − CBP USD) is shown as "—" until there is a USD measure. The value-basis dial's first position is relabelled "Share of US imports".
16. **`psa_` factors are excluded.** "Possibly the Same As" factors are inherited from a record that may not be the same entity. WRO-origin and ASPI-adjacent factors are outside the UFLPA dials and are listed by `build.py` as unmapped.
17. **Freight forwarders count as untraced.** When the shipper of record is a logistics firm (by name pattern in `extract_sayari.py`, 259 entities, 6.7% of shipments), its risk factors describe the forwarder's network, not the goods. Exporters with no inbound trade in Sayari (1.9%) are also untraced. Traced share: 91.3%.
18. **Inline responses saved abridged.** Responses too large for context were copied byte-for-byte from disk. Smaller responses returned inline were saved with every data field and an `abridged` block listing the boilerplate left out (guidance text, risk-factor definitions). Universe pulls are the top 600 module and 300 cell suppliers. The unpulled tails (1,293 module suppliers with ≤5 shipments each, i.e. ≤6,465 shipments, ≤4%; 46 cell suppliers with 1 each) are excluded from the denominator.
19. **Upstream columns come from hub-inbound rollups, not per-exporter tracing.** `get_upstream_supply_chain` truncated HS 2804.61 to 2804 (industrial gases) and errored with a component filter. S1/S2 in `tiers.json` therefore split polysilicon and wafer shipments into the hubs (VNM, IND, IDN, MYS, THA) by the exporting entity's own flags at the default dial setting, weighted by each hub's share of US module shipments. Limitations: HS 3818/2804.61 into IDN include semiconductor-grade material (SUMCO, Hanna Instruments), and Chinese PV majors' own upstream inside China is invisible to cross-border trade data.
20. **Country of exporter.** Taken in this order: legal-form suffix in the name (SDN BHD, PT, Công ty, PTE LTD …) → registry identifier prefix → first non-US country. One manual override (Hanwha Solutions → KOR) is in `pipeline/country_overrides.csv` with its reason.
21. **M7 spot checks substitute entity summaries for `get_record`.** Profiles and summaries expose source names but no record ids, so `get_record` could not be sampled. Six exporters were checked against live summaries: 5 matched the rollup flags exactly, and Waaree's profile lacked its E2 flags (corroboration −1). VSUN (C-022, detention ended in denial) gets +1. Qcells (C-021) gets no uplift because the detained cells were cleared.
22. **C-013 is no longer held.** The intent rule released it automatically because Layer 3 shows a cliff on `trigger_scope` (0% → 13.3% at research-named). The cliff is real but small. India should decide whether the claim's wording still fits.
23. **Simulation model rebuilt as a four-step chain.** The old model multiplied the flagged share by a "true-exposure rate" *and* by (1 − CBP release rate). Both stand for "the goods turned out to be clean", so the loss was counted down twice. With real Layer 3 data, every scenario then removed under 6% of supply and no run ever produced a shortage, so the page showed nothing. The new chain is flagged (Sayari) → stopped (share customs stops) → kept out (1 − release, from CBP) → lost (1 − rerouting). Untraced supply is now scaled from the traced rate at the same reach (× a multiplier, default 1), not added as a fixed 0–12 pp. The shortage formula is unchanged. Each input is a centre value ± spread (symmetric triangular), so a slider can move it. The true-exposure rates (τ) are dropped from the model.
24. **Scenario presets.** Today's rule (T1), Look further back (T2), Look everywhere (T5), and Everywhere and strict (T5, 90% stopped, 25% released, 10% rerouted). The strict values are my illustrative choice, labelled "scenario setting" on the page.
25. **Redesign and plain language.** The purple/periwinkle glass look was dropped for ink navy on paper with a single ochre accent for the shadow market. Kept from the reference: Inter Tight display type, ice-blue square panels with navy type, the vertical-stripe hero and the big-heading + short-lede layout. Dial and category names are plain English in `app.js` (`PLAIN`, `CATS`); `rules/uflpa.json` keeps the technical labels. "Cliff" is called "jump" on the page. The dead "tolerated exposure" metric (no USD values) is replaced with "flagged here but not under today's rule". The hero headline is computed from data: "{today}% flagged. {full depth}% linked."
26. **Product check (on by default).** First Solar's exporting entities make up ~42% of US cell and module shipments in the window, and Sayari links them to forced-labour risk at tiers 2–4 under every scope. First Solar makes cadmium telluride thin-film panels with no polysilicon or silicon wafers, the main route for Xinjiang risk in solar (SI-014, SI-016, SI-017). Seoul Semiconductor Vina ships LED modules under HS 8541.43. `pipeline/product_relevance.csv` sets both aside: their flags are kept (`flags_all`, `combos_unadjusted`) but excluded from the headline figures. This lowers full-depth linked supply from 84% to 40%. The links may still reflect other inputs (glass, aluminium) that UFLPA also covers, so the check can be switched off on both pages and both figures are shown in the hero. Excluding them is the cautious default.
27. **"Today" is the enforcement record, not "direct suppliers".** M7 check 2 showed that the companies CBP has detained sit at E2/E3, so "direct suppliers ≈ current practice" was contradicted by the data. `pipeline/enforced_groups.csv` maps the 7 company groups with a public solar detention or denial (cases.csv) to exporters by name pattern; all 42 matches were checked by hand. They account for 11% of shipments. The simulation's T1 ("Today") flags exactly those groups. The shadow market is now "linked at these settings, with no public detention on record". CBP doesn't name most detained importers, so this is an upper bound on what enforcement has missed. That caveat is on the page.
28. **Estimated weight as a third measure.** Sayari supplier roll-ups carry counts, not weight. The 30 largest exporters (86% of estimated weight) were sampled with `search_shipments` (34 raw files, `data/derived/shipment_samples.csv`). Weight = shipments × the entity's median kg per shipment, with the pooled median for the rest. Caveats: Sayari's date filter did not hold for 8 exporters (out-of-window rows used for the median), some matches were partial, and weights repeat across bill-of-lading lines (the implied total of ~21 Mt is ~8× too high). So only relative shares are shown, never tonnes. Full-depth linked share is 32% by weight against 40% by shipments.
29. **Simulation inputs sourced, and border detention time added.** The inventory buffer (4 months, 1.5–6.5), replacement rate (1.5% of demand per month), supplier delay (9 months) and rerouting (0.3) are now estimates from cited sources (SI-001 to SI-013), badged "estimate from sources". No source gives a 2026 inventory figure or a supplier qualification time, so those centres are our inference from the cited figures. New input: months a stopped shipment is held (2, 0.5–3.5; CBP and PV Tech). Shortage is now the longer of (a) the permanent-loss shortage (unchanged formula) and (b) the hold-up: months held − stock × 100 ÷ stopped share. Only "share customs stops" and "untraced exposure" remain placeholders.
30. **Presentation.** Added a data-driven "What we found" section, so every number is computed from the build. Scenario state lives in the URL (`#simulation?preset=strict`), with a copy-link button. Added About, how-to-cite and download links, and a naming-review panel with `data/derived/named_companies.csv` as the review sheet, plus columns for reviewer and right of reply. Independent review and right of reply are marked outstanding: they need people, not code. Charts carry text alternatives with their numbers. The site payload is slimmed (factor names stay in metrics.json), from 816 KB to ~520 KB.

## 2026-11-16 · Case 2: proposed rule (optical transceivers × FCC Covered List), branch `proposed-rule`
31. **Corrections to the build brief after checking every source.** Each correction is reflected in the cards, claims and source register.
    - **Carr quote.** The "component part loophole" quote is taken from Carr's statement on the adopted rule (PR-005, FCC-26-50A2). In PR-003 (the 1 July proposal release) the phrase is FCC staff text, not Carr's words.
    - **S.5548 date.** Introduced on **24 Sep 2026** (GovInfo, PR-025), not 25 Sep.
    - **I-3 (3.2T phase-in).** Sourced to PR-015, PR-028, PR-013 and PR-019, not PR-012. Cignal (PR-012) argues that 800G/1.6T revisions would count as new, and Fusion rated 1.6T high exposure. That conflict is shown on the card, which is rated low confidence.
    - **I-4 (65% exemption).** Rests on a single reported chain (Economic Daily News citing Morgan Stanley, via PR-015). The "drone 65% precedent" was **dropped**: it is in none of the cited sources and wasn't found elsewhere.
    - **NVIDIA 800G figure.** "InnoLight + Eoptolink ≈ 60% of NVIDIA 800G" is second-hand (PR-016, not in Counterpoint's PR-014). It is noted in the register only and not used for anchors.
    - **I-7 (1260H).** The 188-entity total comes from the DoW release (PR-024). The product ban (30 Jun 2027) excludes components inside end items.
    - **I-9 (routers).** Conditional Approvals end between 1 Oct and 5 Dec 2027.
    - **Security wording.** The brief's line "optics are hardware, not data-collection software" was **dropped**, because modules run firmware on microcontrollers (PR-011). It is replaced by the sourced statement that no incident has been publicly documented (PR-027), shown with the opposing views.
    - **Design-reading alternatives.** The alternative "chosen to reduce litigation risk" had no source. It is replaced by the FCC's own stated reasons: statutory authority and administrability (PR-001).
    - **Source types and titles.** Titles and source types are corrected for PR-004, PR-013 and PR-016 (commercial and vendor sources, not analysts). PR-018's errors are noted, and PR-018 is used only for the capacity point. congress.gov (PR-020) returned 403, so it is kept with "URL not verified at build" and the text was verified at GovInfo.
32. **Supply segments.** Seven illustrative segments with centre values summing to 100: InnoLight 29 (16 China / 13 Thailand), Eoptolink 12 (6/6), other Chinese-HQ makers 18, non-Chinese abroad 33, non-Chinese US-made 8. The anchors are Chinese-HQ ≈ 59%, InnoLight 27–34% and Eoptolink 10–15%. The Eoptolink range is the brief's anchor; no register source gives it. The China/Thailand split is drawn per future from a wide range (InnoLight 45% ± 30, Eoptolink 50% ± 30), so it can enter "What matters most". Generation coverage uses the market-wide demand ramp by year. Each segment's 2026 `gen_mix` is kept as data for students but doesn't change the coverage maths in this version.
33. **Intel confidence → uncertainty.** As specified: a lever set by a medium-confidence card takes its next-most-likely option in 25% of futures, and a low-confidence one in 50%. The alternatives are listed in the JSON (`alt`, `alt_delta`). Levers set by a preset or by hand are fixed. "Later cards override earlier" follows the card list order. I-1, I-7, I-8 and I-9 are primary but set few or no transceiver levers: they are context, and they drive the checklist.
34. **Model choices not fixed by the brief.**
    - **Federal share.** For federal-only scope, the share multiplies coverage at the "covered when it starts" row, not "in the rule's sights", which shows only who the trigger reaches.
    - **Grandfathering.** New-models-only uses a grandfathering ramp (40/75/95/100% of purchases are new models in years 1–4). It treats 3.2T as fully new when the rule starts by 2028, because no 3.2T models exist before then (PR-012).
    - **S.5548 phase-in.** S.5548's "five years after enactment" is modelled as a linear phase-in from an assumed 2027 enactment. That is an upper bound on early effect, and the card says so.
    - **Capacity growth.** Capacity is added from 2027.
    - **Stock bought ahead.** Stock is pull-forward months × the net blocked share in the start year.
    - **Charts.** The tornado and the trigger chart use the typical **peak blocked share after shifting**, not the shortfall, because the shortfall is often zero and would hide which levers matter. The readout shows the shortfall.
    - **"No new rule".** Implemented as a `none` option on the trigger lever.
35. **Shared code.** `sim.js` now exposes `SM.rng`, `SM.triInv`, `SM.normal` and `SM.phi`; Case 1 output is byte-identical for the same seed (checked). On resize, the visible view's SVGs are cleared before redrawing. Previously, old desktop-width charts held containers open, which caused horizontal scroll on narrow screens on both simulation pages.

## 2026-11-19 · Presentation layer: landing preview, supply chain map, playback, compare, story, goal finder
36. **Supply chain map (landing preview and network view).** One circle per exporter, area proportional to US shipments (Sayari). Each exporter's group is computed in the browser with the same rule as `pipeline/build.py` `flagged_under()`. The shares were checked against `combos` and match exactly at every reach (0, 11.04, 22.27, 25.33, 25.47, 40.05%).
    - **Groups.** Exporters are placed by the first reach step that flags them.
    - **Merges for legibility.** The "shared owner" step (0.1% of shipments) is drawn in the "up to 4 steps back" column, and the empty "on the blacklist" column is hidden. Lighting by reach still uses each exporter's own flags, so the "+ owners" button stays exact.
    - **Why not supplier-to-supplier links.** The data holds hub-level upstream roll-ups (`tiers.json`), not per-exporter supplier links, so the map does not draw them. Drawing them would invent connections.
    - **Tooltips.** Each company is named with its link level, confidence, customs action on record and Sayari source, plus the note that a link is not proof of forced labour.
    - **Autoplay.** The 30-second autoplay reads every number from the data. It pauses when the tab is hidden and skips to the end under reduced motion.
37. **"Play it out" uses the simulator's own simplification.** The playback shows the typical future: the median flagged, stopped, kept-out and lost shares, with the slider values as inputs.
    - **The gap.** It is everything stopped while reviews run, then the supply lost for good, until new suppliers reach full volume at ramp + lost ÷ capacity. This is the same assumption `sim.js` makes, so the playback never shows a softer picture than the headline numbers.
    - **Visual only.** The new-supplier "scaling up" bar between qualification and full volume is visual. Stock is not credited until full volume.
    - **Where it differs from `sim.js`.** The playback adds the hold-up and the permanent loss into one stock line, where `sim.js` takes the longer of the two. That can make the playback slightly more severe, and the on-page note says the headline numbers come from the full simulation.
38. **Goal finder definitions.**
    - **"Kept out".** The supply lost to the US for good, as a share of all linked supply (every link the data can see, plus the untraced estimate). It is computed per simulated future and the median is taken.
    - **Levers searched.** Reach (6 levels), share stopped (30–100%), share released (0–90%), stock (4–10 months) and new-supplier capacity (1.5–4.5% a month), with today's value always included. The uncontrolled inputs keep their uncertainty ranges.
    - **Runs.** 3,000 futures per combination, with common random numbers.
    - **"Smallest change".** Ranks combinations by the sum of each lever's move from today, scaled by its range.
    - **Default goal.** Keep out 30%, with no more than a 10% chance of a shortage and no more than a 10% chance of a serious disruption. A 50% goal is unreachable by construction: linked supply is about 44% of all supply, so keeping out half of it loses about 22%, past the 20% serious-disruption line. The page says so in its analysis.
39. **Smooth sliders.** While a slider is dragged, a 2,000-future preview runs once per frame (about 1 ms) and only the fast charts redraw. Letting go runs the full 10,000 futures and the sensitivity test. A Web Worker was not needed: with this split, a measured drag adds no dropped frames. Compare mode pins a scenario as A. While A is pinned, the deltas and the distribution outline refer to A instead of the last setting.
40. **"Play it out" opens on the starting scenario.** The setup screen shows the scenario (presets, what the rule checks, and the five settings that shape the playback) and what happens to every 100 shipments. Changing a setting there runs the real simulator, so the What if? page and the playback always agree. The four outcomes (let straight through, held then released, kept out but back in, kept out for good) use the median flagged, stopped, kept-out and lost shares, and they match the dots' colours in the playback. One dot is 2.5% of a month's supply. The counts on screen are dots, not a separate estimate.
