What the rule catches.What it tolerates.

Every supply chain regulation draws lines: which lists count, how many tiers upstream, what standard of proof. The Shadow Market is the supply that sits inside a rule's intent but outside its enforcement. This tool measures that gap for the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act and US solar imports, and asks why the lines sit where they do.

The same rule, read three ways: as written, as enforced, and as it would apply if every tier of the supply chain were visible.

The data shows where the lines sit. The record (statute, guidance, testimony, lobbying, research) shows why. Each claim carries a type and a confidence level.

Timeline

    Intent rule: a data cliff plus documentary evidence that a boundary was contested or chosen is reported only as evidence consistent with a deliberate choice, never as proof. Alternative explanations are shown with every claim.

    Named suppliers

    Companies are named only with their exposure level, confidence and source. Exposure means a supply chain connection, not proof that a product contains a targeted input.

    If the line moved

    Ten thousand seeded runs per threshold. Uncertain inputs are drawn from sourced ranges: high-confidence flags barely move, low-confidence ones swing widely.
