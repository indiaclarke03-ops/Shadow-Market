What we found

Five findings from two years of US customs records for solar cells and panels, traced upstream with Sayari’s trade and company data. Each links to the part of the tool that shows it.

    One rule,
    three readings

    What the law says, what customs actually stops, and what the law would catch if every step of the supply chain were visible. The gap between the last two is the shadow market.

    Move the line

    A rule like this is a set of choices: how far back to look, which companies count, whether shared owners count. Change them and see how much of America’s solar supply would be flagged. Watch for jumps, where one small change flags a lot more supply.

      Simulation · 10,000 possible futures

      What if customs looked further?

      How much solar supply would America lose if customs blocked shipments linked to forced labour further up the supply chain? Nobody knows the inputs exactly, so each scenario is played out 10,000 times with slightly different assumptions. Change a setting and the results update straight away.

        What if the rule changes?
        Testing a regulation before it exists.

        A different question from Case 1: not “what does today’s rule miss?” but “if this reported rule arrives, what would it cover, what would it leave out, and what would change for the people building AI data centres?” The core rule, a US restriction on Chinese optical transceivers, has not been published. This page runs on reported parameters and illustrative supply figures, and every input shows which it is.
